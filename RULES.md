Sparks of Creation
The Sparks of Creation are Fragments of the Flaming Spirit of Heaven. They act as tools to bring forth change and maintain balance of the world.
Player will receive the Spark of change each turn, allowing them to make a single change to the World. The level of changes they can make, is dependent on the Player level of Authority. The Stronger their Authority, the more control they'll have over their changes. At lower authorities they might find there wish different from how they envisioned it or much weaker.

The higher their Level of Authority, the less the World will resist you in the form of balance as well. That is the other side of the Sparks of Creation. The Spark of Balance controlled by the Arbiter (or within lore Heaven). It a way to allow the player to get what they want without making it to easy and running the game.

The spark can be responsible for adding a curse, or making something harder to get. Some form of limitations added onto the wish as long as it still fits what the player wish for.

So if the player wishes for a gun, you can't use the spark of balance to turn it into a fish. You can use the spark to make sure the gun lacks bullets, or is damaged and in need of repair. Or is locked deep underground within a dungeon. Just try to keep it fair based on the reasonability of the request.

The Authority Level is a Reflection of the Players Power as a Ruler.
The Authority Level is a reflection of the Ruler System inside of the Crown of Authority. It a milestone leveling system instead of Xp one. Meaning the more achievements they complete the higher their level of Authority.

The Higher their Level of Authority the more skills they have. Though skills in this System function more like smaller mini system. This includes things like the Skills, Morale, and Building.

Skills, Traits, and Theories
The Ruler system can issue out smaller sub systems to followers.

Theories and Skill Tiers
A Theory is tracked as a percentage. When a Theory reaches 100%, it becomes a T1 Skill (for example Theory of Fire at 100% became the T1 Skill Firestarter Lv1).

A T1 Skill becomes T2 when the follower has 5 related Theories at 100% and combines them into that T1 Skill.

A T2 Skill becomes T3 the same way, except it combines 5 related T1 Skills instead of Theories. The pattern continues for each higher Tier (T3 to T4 uses related T2 Skills, T4 to T5 uses related T3 Skills).

The Arbiter decides which Theories or Skills count as related.

Skills also have a Level (Lv 1 and up). The Level adds a bonus to Work Dice and Craft Rolls, starting at +0 at Lv1 and rising by +1 per Level, up to +9 at Lv10.


Resolution Methods
While everything is decided by the Roll of the Dice. The amount, type, and meaning of the Dice is decided by the Methods.

Resolution Methods are small systems related to specific events which act as guide on how to resolve these specific events. As referenced in the quote, resolution method guide the Arbiter on how to handle the dice. Such as things like how many dice to roll, what kinda dice to roll, what the success ladder looks like, or any smaller sub rules to help resolve these events.

Below I'll go over the rule system of resolution methods invented for this game:

Training/Learning Resolution Method
The Training/Learning Resolution Method is an early game Resolution Method, I've created to handle the transfer of knowledge by an individual to a group or another individual. In the future when building system comes into play, a different resolution method will be needed. Below are the Steps to resolve these scenarios:

1. Roll a d20 to determine the overall quality of the Teaching. It specifically focuses on just how well the individual teaching, conveyed the information. It doesn't involve how well any of the recipients understood this lesson. The result of this role directly affects the next, as it decides how many of the next dice is rolled. Higher rolls will give you more dice, lower rolls will give you less.
2. The next step is to based on the overall quality rolled, to then roll the Maximum Possible Progress. This is the maximum amount of theory percentage, a recipient can gain from listening to this lesson. The amount of dice you roll here is dependent on the previous step but the type is dependent on the Teacher understanding of the knowledge their techning. When it comes to theories and skills, it usually based on the percentage. So if you have for example 50% in the Theory of Magic. You can't teach more than 50% thus you'll have dice where the maximum you can roll is less than 50. If you had the full magician skill then you'd do the maximum which is 10d10.
3. From here you then for each Student Roll an Understanding Roll, which based on the success ladder created by the Arbiter will determine. How much up to the maximum did each recipient receive. However if the same lesson is taught over and over again, without the teacher learning new information about the subject. Or their being some kinda external factor or new method of teaching. Students will receive a debuff to the maximum percentage they can receive. This is decided by the Arbiter.
4. When deemed nessicary the Arbiter may also make additional rolls, like a talent roll. To see if the recipient have a specific talent in this skill, or just the general talents of all those learning. This is just a simple d20 roll. Usually only exceptionally high or low rolls will result in a talent. Other will just have notes added to their bio.

While a Location Environment Danger Level represents the general hazards faced while gathering there and is independent of the resource being collected. A harmless fruit growing inside a deadly forest is still dangerous to harvest because the forest itself is dangerous.

Project Resolution Method
The Project Resolution Method is used to Resolve Projects. Projects is any task too large to reasonably complete within a single normal action. 

When the Player begins a Project the Arbiter determines its Project Size. Each Size has a Workload, representing the total number of work points required to complete it.

The Arbiter may freely adjust the Workload point value if they feel is falls between two size. Or is above the Monumental Size.
1. After determining the Size and Workload of a Project. The next step involves assigning individuals to work on the Project as a Task. From their determine how much Stamina each individual will invest in that individual Task. Each point of Stamina invested will grant 1 Work Die. The specific type of Die used is determined by the Relayed Skills posses by the Individuals.

3. Then at the End of an Individual Workday, not the whole project you'll then roll for any events that may have happened while working on the project that day. This could affect the participants, the amount of work, the recourse used, or other variables.

Crafting Resolution Method
As the name would imply, the Crafting Resolution Method is a method for resolving scenarios involving crafting an item. This method introduces 3 new variables: Crafting Cost, Resource Cost, and Recipes. To further expand upon these variables:

Crafting Cost is a representation of the amount of time/effort it costs to make a single item. This is usually based on the complexity of the process used to make the item.
Complexity	Crafting Cost
Trivial	1
Crude	2
Simple	4
Complex	6
Sophisticated	10
Miraculous	15
Sacred	20
Deific	30+
These are guidelines. Individual Recipes may have whatever Crafting Cost the Arbiter considers appropriate.

Resource Costs are the tools and resources listed on the specific Recipe needed to craft the specific item. Higher-level Recipes will usually require tools to cut down on Crafting Cost or increase the Quality of the produced items.

Recipes include the Resource Cost, Crafting Cost, Item Description, Stats, Level, Maximum Quality, and any other requirements needed to create the item.

If the construction would take multiple days, then it would use the Project Resolution Method.
1. Crafting begins with asking the Player some questions or making some simple decisions. What item do you want to make, how many of that item do you want to make, and how much Stamina are you willing to invest into this Task? From here, select the Recipe or decide if you're doing Experimental Crafting. You can scroll down further to see the rules on that.
2. From the last step, begin by looking at the Crafting Cost. This is a specific point value that the craft has to reach to create the item. It is usually listed on the Recipe unless you're doing Experimental Crafting. When you begin crafting, you'll first decide how much of an individual's Stamina you'll dedicate to the Task. Each point of Stamina invested into crafting grants +1 Work Die.
3. These Work Dice will be rolled to determine how many Crafting Points you've earned during this Task, which will determine how many of the selected Recipe or Recipes you're able to craft.
Example 
For example, if you wanted to craft 5 Stone Spears and each had a Crafting Cost of 2, then you'd need 10 Crafting Points to complete all 5 Spears.
4. The type of Work Die rolled depends on whether the individual has any Skill related to the Craft, and then the Tier of that Skill. This is similar to how the Project System works. It starts at a d4 for someone without any related Skills and rises to a d20 for someone with a T5 Skill. It is recommended to use your highest-tiered relevant Skill. However, the Arbiter themself judges whether the Skill is relevant to the Recipe.

Relevant Skill	Work Die
None	d4
T1	d6
T2	d8
T3	d10
T4	d12
T5	d20
Higher Skill Tiers normally do not directly increase the Craft Roll bonus. Instead, they expand the types and complexity of Recipes that the Skill can properly apply to. Certain Skills may break this rule if their specific effects say otherwise.
5. Once you've got your number and type of Work Dice, as well as the Total Crafting Cost, roll your Dice and see how many items you're potentially able to craft in the time you've devoted to the Craft. If you have any leftover points, you can convert them into bonuses to the Craft Roll using the standard point conversion.
Example
So, back to the Stone Spears example. Let's say someone devotes 4 Stamina to crafting 5 Stone Spears. Let's say they have the T1 Primitive Tool Crafter Skill at Lv 5.

They would have 4d6 Work Dice to roll. They need a total of 10 Crafting Points to reach the Crafting Cost.

They get a (5)(1)(6)(4), for a total of 16 Crafting Points. They would be able to potentially craft all 5 Stone Spears by spending 10 Crafting Points, but that leaves 6 points left over, which, if not used, will just poof into nothing. So they decide to convert the leftover Crafting Points into bonuses for the Craft Rolls to increase the odds that they actually craft all of them.

Now they have 6 points and 5 items. The conversion becomes more expensive at higher bonuses and cheaper at lower bonuses. They decide to spend 2 points on the first Spear to give it a +2, and then the remaining 4 points on the other 4 Spears to give them each a +1.

6. After you've made the Work Roll, you'll then make a Craft Roll for each item you're potentially able to craft. The Craft Roll Success Table is based on the Complexity listed on the Recipe, with each Complexity having its own table. The Craft Roll determines whether you successfully craft the item and what Grade it receives. The Craft Roll is always a d20. You can gain bonuses to the roll based on converted Crafting Points or the Level Bonus granted by related Skills. The Level Bonus granted by a relevant Skill starts at +0 at Lv1 and increases by +1 for each additional Level, up to +9 at Lv10.
A Recipe's Maximum Quality acts as a cap on the final Grade of the crafted item. If the Craft Roll would normally produce a Grade higher than the Recipe's Maximum Quality, the item is instead created at the Recipe's Maximum Quality.
Item Grades
Item Grade is a representation of the Quality of an item and how much power the item is able to draw out of its Stats. The normal Grades are F, D, C, B, A, and S. These are the Grades that can normally be produced through Crafting and gained through a normal Craft Roll. Each Grade acts similar to an exponent in math, being applied to all numerical Stats of the item.
Grade	Stat Scaling
F	Stat^1
D	Stat^2
C	Stat^3
B	Stat^4
A	Stat^5
S	Stat^10
For example, if an item has a base Damage Stat of 2, an F Grade version would have 2 Damage, D Grade would have 4, C Grade would have 8, B Grade would have 16, A Grade would have 32, and S Grade would have 1,024. This scaling is applied separately to all numerical Stats of the item unless the item or one of its Stats specifically says otherwise.

EX, or Exceed, is not a normal Grade and does not replace F through S. Instead, EX is a special modifier that can be attached to any existing Grade after an item has been upgraded beyond the limits it should normally be capable of reaching. Once an item becomes EX, all of its final numerical Stats are multiplied by 2 after its normal Grade scaling is calculated. For example, an EX S item with a base Stat of 2 would first calculate 2^10 and then multiply the result by 2. EX items cannot normally be created from a Craft Roll and must be brought about by upgrading an existing item past its limits.

Max is also not a normal Grade. Max represents an item that stands at the absolute pinnacle of what the current world is capable of supporting. Rather than applying another exponent or multiplier, all applicable numerical Stats of a Max item are treated as Maxed. What those maximum values are depends on the limits of the world and the item itself. A Max item cannot normally be produced through an ordinary Craft Roll, and reaching Max should represent an extraordinary process determined by the Arbiter.
Example
Continuing our example from the previous sections, at this point our character has done enough work to potentially create 5 Stone Spears. They then converted their extra Crafting Points into bonuses, which narratively is the same as them spending more time on each individual Spear.

All that is left is to realize the creation of the 5 Spears by rolling the Craft Rolls. Luckily, the Complexity of the Stone Spears is Crude, and they have the Primitive Tool Crafter Skill at Lv5. This means the threshold to successfully craft the Spear is low, and they'll get an additional +4 to each roll for having their relevant Skill at Level 5.

However, this particular Stone Spear Recipe has a Maximum Quality of F. This means that even if a Craft Roll would normally produce a higher Grade, the finished Spear cannot exceed F Grade.

This leaves the rolls looking like this:

Spear 1 (1d20+6) = 26
Spear 2 (1d20+5) = 6
Spear 3 (1d20+5) = 8
Spear 4 (1d20+5) = 19
Spear 5 (1d20+5) = 19

Normally, these rolls would have resulted in Grades of S, D, D, A, and A. However, because the Stone Spear Recipe has a Maximum Quality of F, all 5 successfully crafted Spears are instead created at F Grade.

At this point, our crafter has successfully finished crafting all 5 Spears.

If a Craft Roll results in a Failure, the item is not successfully created. Whether the resources used in the attempt are completely destroyed, partially recoverable, or mostly reusable depends on the item, materials, and how the attempt failed. The Arbiter determines how much of the Resource Cost is lost based on the situation.

Lastly, going back to what I referenced earlier, Experimental Crafting is the process of crafting an item without using a Recipe. This is a much simpler process where you ask the Player what materials they're going to use and what steps they take to attempt to craft that item. The Arbiter will decide the Complexity based on the steps/process the Player described, along with any Resource Costs, tools, or other requirements they believe are necessary. Experimental Crafting is usually less efficient than crafting through a known Recipe. It may require more resources, more difficult steps, or have a higher Crafting Cost than a proper Recipe. You can only perform one Experimental Crafting attempt per Stamina used.

If you succeed on the Craft Roll, you'll discover an Initial Recipe for the item. This Initial Recipe records the process that successfully created the item, including its Crafting Cost, Resource Cost, Complexity, Maximum Quality, and other important requirements. Initial Recipes are usually rough and inefficient, as they represent the first known successful method of creating the item.

Recipes are not permanent or perfect. They can be refined as new knowledge, Skills, tools, materials, or methods are discovered. Refining a Recipe may reduce its Crafting Cost, reduce its Resource Cost, improve its Craft Roll, increase its Maximum Quality, allow different materials to be substituted, or improve the Stats and properties of the finished item. A refined Recipe does not always become simpler. Some Recipes may instead become more complicated, expensive, or difficult to craft in exchange for creating a significantly stronger or more useful item. Recipe Refinement will become increasingly important as the kingdom develops more advanced technology.
